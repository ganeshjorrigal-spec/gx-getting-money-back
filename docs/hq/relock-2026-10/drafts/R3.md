# IDEA LOCK · Build Sprint (re-lock, 10 Oct 2026)

Labels: VERIFIED (official text or the company's own page, opened by us). REPORTED (news, public posts). OBSERVATION (what I saw myself, small samples). MISSING (not known yet, with the date I get it). A date marked "Tickback's expectation" is our choice, never a law.

**The idea in one line:**
An AI agent that takes over a stuck domestic flight refund in India and stays on it till the money lands. It tells you who owes the refund and by which date under the DGCA rule, writes every mail for you to send from your own Gmail, reads every reply from the travel site and the airline, and moves you up to the right officer when they stall.

v1 is one case: a domestic flight booked on MakeMyTrip, Goibibo, Cleartrip or EaseMyTrip (or direct with the airline), cancelled, refund not landed. The landing page, the demo, my outreach and my tests are flights only. Online-store refunds are built after flights in the same milestone as a second playbook on the same agent, and are not on the landing page. Event tickets, where I started, stay live but not sold. Out of v1: international flights, compensation claims, hotels, trains, EPF, chargebacks, legal notices, voice calls, logging in for you, OTPs, bank details.

Honest first: I have no stuck flight refund of my own and I have not met a person with one yet. My own refunds (Rapido Rs 100, Swiggy Rs 210) were promised within a day, which is why I left them.
- Data nobody else has (partial): a flight refund playbook built from the DGCA text itself (refund rules revised 24 Feb 2026, in force 26 Mar 2026), every rule quoted with link and date, and the named Nodal Officer and Appellate Authority for IndiGo, Air India, SpiceJet and Akasa plus the grievance officers of MakeMyTrip, Goibibo, Cleartrip and EaseMyTrip, each read on the company's own page and checked by a separate verifier agent (VERIFIED).

## GOAL

**The one goal they hire it for (money, time, status or life):**
Money. My flight money back from whoever actually has it, without me becoming the messenger.

**Delta 4 (the steps today → the steps with my product):**
Today, about 10 steps (from the playbook and 29 public X complaints, REPORTED, not my own case):
1. Cancellation mail; app says "refund initiated"
2. Wait, check the bank, nothing
3. App chatbot repeats a template
4. Post on X; get "please DM us" (17 of 29 posts)
5. Send booking ID in DM; "under review"; quiet
6. Google the rule; blogs give old day counts
7. Hunt for the Nodal Officer or grievance officer email
8. Write a long mail, guess the proof
9. Forget the date; open the bank app every morning
10. AirSewa or 1915, or give up

With Tickback, 4 steps:
1. Paste the cancellation mail or a screenshot
2. See who owes it, by when, under which rule, and who sits above them
3. Tap to send from my own Gmail (Tickback's case inbox in CC)
4. When anyone replies, or the date comes, the next mail is ready; on the day, "It's in" or "Not yet"

Why one ChatGPT prompt doesn't do this: it can write the first mail. It never sees the airline's or travel site's reply, cannot change who it writes to when a reply says the other company has the money, does not come back on a date computed in code, and does not carry the dated thread up the ladder. Every case ends with a count of who did what: "You: 4 taps. Tickback: read 3 replies, set 3 dates, wrote 4 mails."

**The sin it rides (optional):**
Wrath: they passed me around and kept my money, now one of them answers in writing.

## USER

**Who exactly:**
Someone in India who flies domestic a few times a year, books on a travel site for the deal, pays by card or UPI, whose flight got cancelled, and whose refund has not landed by the date they were told. Why this person: big direct airline refunds clear under public pressure (IndiGo's December 2025 mass cancellations, REPORTED), and small app refunds clear in a day. Money gets stuck in the long tail: the travel-site leg and smaller airlines (two SpiceJet-via-Goibibo posts at 90 and 120 days, REPORTED). Why flights before stores: for flights the law fixes both the owner and the date (travel-site booking: the airline owes it within 14 working days; credit card, 7 days; VERIFIED). For online stores the rule only says "a reasonable period".

**The trigger (when the pain hits):**
The first "not yet": the app says "refund initiated" or "processed" and the bank shows nothing (7 of 29 posts), or support moves me to DM and goes quiet (17 of 29) (REPORTED, signals not rates).

Public proof that money gets stuck in the middle: a Bilaspur consumer commission order (18 Jul 2026) found Go Airlines had refunded Rs 6,437 to Paytm for a flight cancelled in 2020, the passenger never got it, and Paytm was held liable (REPORTED, Outlook Business, 22 Jul 2026).

## PRODUCT

**Onboarding (how a first-time user feels the value fastest):**
No sign-up. Paste the cancellation mail, the travel site's reply or a screenshot. The agent shows what it understood (airline, booked on, payment method, cancellation date) to confirm, asks the PNR, and in under a minute shows the "who owes you" card: who, by when, under which rule, who sits above them, and the first mail ready, or "nothing to send yet, it is on time" with the date on your calendar. No live case? "Try a demo": a labelled demo travel site and demo airline reply in your own Gmail thread over 3 rounds.

**The core loop (user stories):**
(Written from public posts until my first real case. I rewrite it in that person's words by Sun 11 night and say whose it is.)

When my flight got cancelled and the app is showing "refund initiated" since many days, I want to know who is actually having my money and by which date it has to come, so that I can stop running behind the chatbot and stop opening my bank app every morning.
Push: the bot gives the same line again and again, on X they say please DM, I send my booking ID and then nothing.
Anxiety: if I don't keep following up I think the money just sits with them, and it is not a small amount for me. Every blog is saying a different number of days, and if I write something wrong, what if they close my complaint?

1. My flight got cancelled and the app says refund initiated. I paste the mail. Tickback tells me I booked on a travel site, so the airline has to finish my refund in 14 working days, and the date is in my calendar. Nothing to send now, it is on time. Honestly that is a relief.
2. The date passed and nothing came. Tickback has one mail ready to the travel site's grievance officer, with the airline's Nodal Officer in the same thread, and it asks one plain question: "On which date did the airline pay this refund to you?" I read it and send it from my Gmail.
3. A reply comes. If the airline says it already paid the travel site, Tickback switches: now the travel site owes me, and the next mail asks for the bank reference. If the airline has not paid, it stays with the airline and the next mail goes to its Appellate Authority. I don't decode anything myself.
4. They give a reference number. Tickback tells me straight that the money has left them and the last leg is my bank, and gives me what to tell the bank. Sometimes the honest answer is the other side is right.
5. Still nothing after the officers. The AirSewa complaint is ready with the whole dated thread. When it lands I tap "It's in" and see the whole chase.

**Coming back (optional):**
The case brings you back: a calendar alert when a reply comes, check-ins on the computed dates, one case link with the full thread.

**The AI-first part (onboarding, engagement or the core loop):**
The core loop. For every reply on the thread, the model reads which company it points at, what it claims (stall, under review, waiting on the other company, paid on a date, processed with a reference, credit shell offered), and any date or reference. Code takes that and the verified playbook and decides the owner, the date, the next person and the next rung. Rules and contacts come from the playbook, never from the model. The playbook is the fuel; the agent working the thread is the product. Without the agent it is a rules page; without the playbook it is ChatGPT guessing, and I saw a strong model guess wrong. You send every mail yourself on purpose: the complaint should come from the passenger's own address. If real cases show drafts going unsent, one-tap send is the next build.
