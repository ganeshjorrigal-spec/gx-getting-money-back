## PRODUCT

**Onboarding:**
No sign-up. One box: paste the cancellation mail, the travel site's reply, or a screenshot. The agent shows "what we understood" (airline, booked on, payment method, cancellation date) to confirm, and asks the PNR and booking ID only at that step. In under a minute the first screen is the "who owes you" card:
- Who owes you: the company the rule names (travel-site booking: the airline, 3(c)).
- By when: the date, with its source. Rule dates carry the rule. Our own check dates say "Tickback's expectation".
- Under which rule: one line, the quote one tap away.
- Who sits above them: the named officer, from the company's own page.
- The one question that pins it (see the core loop).
Then the first message, ready, or "nothing to send yet, it is on time" with the date on my calendar.

For anyone without a live case: "Try a demo". One demo email account plays two labelled roles, "Refund desk · demo (travel site role)" and "Refund desk · demo (airline role)", through the same real path (your Gmail sends, the case inbox reads). Round 1: the travel site stalls. The agent writes one mail to both with the pinning question. Round 2: the airline replies on the same thread that it paid the travel site on a date, and the card flips the owner to the travel site. "Skip ahead 10 days". Round 3: the travel site sends a refund reference. "Money landed" closes it and shows the "Who did what" count. In the demo the replies and the agent's reading of them are scripted; it shows the path, not a refund result. The count on a real case is the real one.

**The core loop (user stories):**

The job, in my words. I wrote this from the public posts, because I do not have my own flight case; I rewrite it from the first real person's words by Sun 11 night and say which case it came from.
When my flight is cancelled and I had booked it on a travel site, and the app is showing "refund initiated" since many days, I want to know who is actually having my money and by which date it has to come, so that I can stop running between two companies and stop opening my bank app every morning.
Push: the app bot gives the same template again and again, on X they only say please DM us, and then nothing. Somebody says it is with the airline, but the airline I never even wrote to.
Anxiety: if I don't keep following up I think the money just sits with them, and it is not a small amount for me. I don't even know the rule, every blog is saying a different number of days. And if I write something wrong, what if they use it to close my complaint?

User flow story: My flight got cancelled by the airline, I had booked on the travel site because it was cheaper. App said refund initiated. One week, two weeks, nothing in the account. I chatted, the bot gave me the same line. I tweeted, they said please DM, I sent my booking ID, they said under review. Honestly I did not know whom to write after this, so I just kept checking the bank every morning, and I think slowly I was giving up on it.

User flow: 1. Flight cancelled, mail came 2. App says refund initiated 3. Ten days, nothing in the account 4. Bot, then X, then please DM, then quiet 5. Paste the mail in Tickback 6. See who owes me, the date and the rule 7. Date passes, tap send on one mail to the travel site and the airline together 8. Airline replies it paid the travel site 9. Next mail to the travel site is ready, I send it 10. Travel site gives a reference number 11. Check the bank with that number 12. Money landed, case closed.

The stories:
1. My flight got cancelled and the app says refund initiated. I paste the mail and I see it plainly: I booked on a travel site, so the airline holds the onus and the refund has to complete in 14 working days (3(c)). The rule gives no start date, so Tickback counts from my cancellation date and tells me so. It says nothing to send now, it is on time, and the date is in my calendar. Honestly that is a relief, I am not chasing for nothing.
2. The date passed and the travel site keeps saying "under review". I don't argue with anyone. Tickback has one mail ready to the travel site's grievance officer and the airline's Nodal Officer on the same thread, with one question: "On which date did the airline pay you this refund?" I tap send from my own Gmail.
3. The airline replies to all that it refunded the travel site on a date. Tickback reads it, and the card flips: now the travel site owes me, and I can see why. For MakeMyTrip or Goibibo, their own terms say they try to pass refunds on within 24 hours of getting them from the airline, so the next mail quotes the airline's reply and that line, and asks for the ARN or UTR. If the airline says it has not paid yet, the airline stays the owner and the next rung is its Appellate Authority.
4. The travel site sends an ARN and a date. Now the money has left them, and Tickback tells me straight: the travel site is right this time, the last leg is my bank, here is what to tell the bank with this number. Sometimes the honest answer is that the other side is right.
5. They offer me a credit shell instead of cash. The reply is ready: thank you, but I want it to my original payment, a credit shell is the passenger's choice (3(f)).
6. I cancelled myself on a non-refundable fare and they kept everything. Tickback tells me what I can honestly ask back: taxes and airport fees always come back (3(d)); a travel-agent fee shown to me at booking does not (3(i)), so it never promises me that.
7. Nothing after the grievance officer and the Nodal Officer. The next rung is ready: Appellate Authority with the full thread, then AirSewa, then the National Consumer Helpline. Tickback cannot file for me and says so. When the money lands I tap "It's in" and I see the whole chase: how many days, and "You: N taps. Tickback: read N replies, named the owner N times, set N dates, wrote N messages."

How the dates work, so nothing is made up: credit card on a direct booking, 7 days (rule); travel-site booking, 14 working days (rule; start date is our anchor, labelled); debit card, UPI or net banking on a direct booking, a check date at 15 working days, labelled Tickback's expectation, never DGCA (D-029); MakeMyTrip and Goibibo pass-on, 24 hours from the airline's refund date (their own terms). Cleartrip and EaseMyTrip publish no pass-on time, so the agent asks for the date and the ARN and invents no deadline. The agent never promises the 48-hour free cancellation on a travel-site booking. Dates are computed in code, never by the model.

**Coming back (optional):**
Not habit; the case brings you back. A calendar alert when the travel site or the airline replies, check-ins on the computed dates, and one case link with the whole thread and proof. Paste stays as the fallback when a company replies only in its app or drops the CC.

**The AI-first part:**
The core loop. For every reply that reaches the case inbox (or that I paste), the model answers three things: which company is this pointing at; what is it claiming (a stall, "under review", waiting on the other company, paid to the other company on a date, processed with or without a reference, credit shell offered, asks for details); and is there a date or reference in it. Code takes those answers and the verified playbook and decides the owner by rule (the owner flip), the date, the next recipient and the next rung. The rule text in each message comes from the playbook row, not from the model. Without the AI it is a rules page I must apply alone. Without the playbook it is ChatGPT guessing the rule, and I have seen a strong model guess it wrong.
The user sends every mail on purpose (Level 1, D-025): the complaint comes from the passenger's own address, which is what the officer answers to, and Tickback never needs to touch anyone's inbox. If live cases show many drafts never sent, one-tap send (Level 2) is the next build.
What I can't claim yet, and what you will see: the CC inbox catches only replies that reply-all, and the share caught is unmeasured. By Fri 16 I report, from live flight cases, replies caught by CC vs pasted, and drafts written vs sent.
