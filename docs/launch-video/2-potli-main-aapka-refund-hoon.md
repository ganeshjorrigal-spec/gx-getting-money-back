## A. The idea in one paragraph

Your stuck flight refund is a small maroon velvet potli with button eyes, the kind a shagun gift comes in. It turns to the camera and speaks to you, its owner: "हैलो? मैं आपका रिफ़ंड हूँ।" It has sat a month on a steel bench between an AIRLINE door and a TRAVEL APP door, been told "Airline से पूछिए" and "हमने भेज दिया", had a "Refund Processed ✓" sticker slapped on it without moving an inch, and watched you retell the story a seventh time. Then we see your face as you put your phone face down and say "छोड़ो यार। जाने दो।", and the potli says the true line of the film: "किसी ने मना नहीं किया। बस आप थक गए।" Its light goes out. In the dark, your hand reaches in and turns the phone back over. Four held, real app states show what changes: Refund Genie says who owes you and by when; Genie writes the mail, you press Send in your own Gmail; the reply is read and the next mail, now to the airline, is ready, because the follow-up, when and to whom, is remembered for you. The light comes back, your face relaxes, and the potli answers your surrender: "अब मुझे जाने मत देना।" It rests on the strongest evidence we have: in five conversations nobody was refused, they were outlasted (OBSERVATION); "refund processed" with no money is a top public complaint (REPORTED, 7 of 29) and so is the travel site pointing at the airline (REPORTED, 4 of 15 flight posts); every caller retells the story from the start (OBSERVATION); the DGCA rule puts the airline on the hook at 14 working days (VERIFIED). The core is fatigue, not anger (INFERENCE), so the promise is not money; it is that you no longer carry the follow-up alone. Nothing is shown beyond what is live, and you press Send.

## C. Storyboard

Sources: LIVE = shot on Ganesh's phone. SCREEN = screen recording of the sample case or the Gmail it opens. AI = Higgsfield or Google Flow (route C2). ### C0. The potli look (applies to both routes)
- Material: deep maroon velvet (or silk) with an antique-gold zari border at the neck, twisted maroon-and-gold cord. Think shagun potli from a wedding, not a craft-class pouch.
- Shape: filled firm with rice in a plastic bag so it sits round and upright; steam out every crease.
- Eyes: two small (10 to 12 mm) glossy black buttons, set a little wide and level, one third down. Big buttons close together read as a baby toy; small and wide read as a calm adult.
- Light: a warm lamp (2700 K) as the key at 45 degrees, at button height, so each button gets one catchlight. The cold tube light is only the backlight and the rim. Never flat overhead light.
- Voice: adult, dry, close to the mic. It sounds like a tired friend telling you something true over chai, with a little humour in "मैं तो हिला भी नहीं".
- **Three-friend gate (day 0, pass or fail):** take one photo of the lit potli, lay the hook caption on it, add the test VO line, play it on a phone at arm's length to three friends aged 25 to 40. Ask "ये किसका ad है, किसके लिए?" Fail if anyone says "bachchon wala", "cute", "cartoon" or "kids". Fail means the potli shots go to the AI route (C2) or a fresh potli; faces and screens do not change.
