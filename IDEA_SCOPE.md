# IDEA LOCK · Build Sprint (re-lock, 10 Oct 2026)

Labels: VERIFIED (official text or the company's own page, opened by us). REPORTED (news or public posts). OBSERVATION (what I saw myself, small samples). MISSING (not known yet, with when I get it). A date marked "Tickback's expectation" is our choice, never a law.

**The idea in one line:**
An AI agent that takes over a domestic flight refund that is past its date and stays on it till the money lands. It tells you who owes the refund and when it was due, writes every mail for you to send from your own Gmail, reads the replies from the travel site and the airline, and moves you up to the right officer when they stall.

v1 is one case: a domestic flight booked on MakeMyTrip, Goibibo, Cleartrip, EaseMyTrip or direct with the airline, cancelled by the airline or by me, and the refund has not landed by its date. The landing page, the demo, my outreach and my tests are flights only. Online-store refunds are a second playbook on the same agent, built only after the flight flow passes a live run, and not on the landing page. Event tickets, where I started, stay live but are not sold. Out of v1: international flights, compensation claims, hotels, trains, EPF, chargebacks, legal notices, voice calls, logging in for you, OTPs, bank details.

**Why me (at least 1 of 3: an audience that trusts me, years inside the workflow, data nobody else has):**
Honest first: I have no stuck flight refund of my own and I have not met a person with one yet. My own refunds (Rapido Rs 100, Swiggy Rs 210) were promised within a day, which is why I left them.
- Data nobody else has (partial): a flight refund playbook built from the DGCA text itself (refund rules revised 24 Feb 2026, in force 26 Mar 2026), every rule quoted with link and date, plus the named Nodal Officer and Appellate Authority of IndiGo, Air India, SpiceJet and Akasa and the grievance officers of MakeMyTrip, Goibibo, Cleartrip and EaseMyTrip, each read on the company's own page and checked by a separate verifier agent (VERIFIED).
- Why it matters: a Gemini Deep Research run on the same job quoted the old rule in 6 of its 10 load-bearing lines (21 working days for travel-site bookings; it is 14 now) and listed Yatra's investor-relations officer as the passenger contact (OBSERVATION, one run).
- Years inside the workflow: no. Audience that trusts me: weak (ex-Performics friends, Isha meditator friends, my institute).
- What I can show: a live agent that already reads company replies through a CC'd case inbox, and a 3-minute demo where a demo company replies inside your own Gmail thread (live for events).

## GOAL

**The one goal they hire it for (money, time, status or life):**
Money. My flight money back from whoever actually has it, without me becoming the messenger between the travel site and the airline.

**Delta 4 (the steps today → the steps with my product):**
Today, about 10 steps (from the playbook and 13 public flight complaints on X, REPORTED, not my own case):
1. Cancellation mail; app says "refund initiated"
2. Weeks pass, I check the bank, nothing
3. App chatbot repeats the same template
4. Post on X; they reply "please DM us" (7 of 13 flight posts)
5. Send booking ID in DM; "under review"; quiet
6. Google the rule; blogs give old day counts
7. Hunt for the Nodal Officer or grievance officer email
8. Write a long mail, guess the proof
9. Forget the date; open the bank app every morning
10. AirSewa or 1915, or give up

With Tickback, 4 steps:
1. Paste the cancellation mail or a screenshot
2. See the "who owes you" card: who, when it was due, which rule, who sits above them, and the next mail ready
3. Open it in my own Gmail, read it, press send, with Tickback's case inbox in CC
4. When anyone replies, or the next date comes, the next mail is ready; on the day, "It's in" or "Not yet"

Why one ChatGPT prompt doesn't do this: it can write the first mail. It sees the airline's or the travel site's reply only if I go back and paste it, cannot switch who it writes to when a reply says the other company has the money, does not come back on a date worked out in code, and does not carry the dated thread up the ladder. Every case ends with a count of who did what, for example "You: 4 taps. Tickback: read 3 replies, set 3 dates, wrote 4 mails."

## PRODUCT

**Onboarding (how a first-time user feels the value fastest):**
No sign-up. Paste the cancellation mail, the travel site's reply or a screenshot. The agent shows what it understood (airline, booked on, payment method, cancellation date) to confirm, asks the PNR, and shows the "who owes you" card (target: under a minute). No live case? "Try a demo": a labelled demo travel site and demo airline reply in your own Gmail thread over 3 rounds.

**The core loop (user stories):**
(Written from public posts until my first real case. I rewrite it in that person's words by Sun 11 night and say whose case it is.)

When my flight got cancelled and the app is showing "refund initiated" since many weeks, I want to know who is actually having my money and when it should have come, so that I can stop running behind the chatbot and stop opening my bank app every morning.
Push: the bot gives the same line again and again, on X they say please DM, I send my booking ID and then nothing.
Anxiety: if I don't keep following up I think the money just sits with them, and it is not a small amount for me. Every blog is saying a different number of days, and if I write something wrong, what if they close my complaint?

1. My refund is late. I paste the cancellation mail. Tickback tells me I booked on a travel site, so the airline has to finish the refund in 14 working days, and counted from my cancellation date (Tickback's reading, the rule gives no start day) it was due last week. One mail is ready to the travel site's grievance officer, with the airline's Nodal Officer on the same thread, asking one plain question: "On which date did the airline pay this refund to you?" I read it and send it from my Gmail.
2. A reply comes. If the airline says it already paid the travel site, Tickback switches: now the travel site has my money, and the next mail asks them for the bank reference. If the airline has not paid, it stays with the airline and the next mail goes to its Appellate Authority. I don't decode anything myself.
3. They give a reference number. Tickback tells me straight that the money has left them and the last leg is my bank, and gives me what to tell the bank. Sometimes the honest answer is the other side is right.
4. Still nothing after the officers: the AirSewa complaint is ready with the dated thread. When the money lands I tap "It's in" and see the whole chase in one count.

**Coming back (optional):**
The case brings you back: a calendar alert when a reply comes, check-ins on the dates worked out in code, one case link with the full thread.

**The AI-first part (onboarding, engagement or the core loop):**
The core loop. For every reply on the thread, the model reads which company it points at, what it claims (stall, under review, waiting on the other company, paid on a date, processed with a reference, credit shell offered), and any date or reference. Code takes that and the verified playbook and decides who owes it, the date, the next person and the next rung. Rules and contacts come from the playbook, never from the model. The playbook is the fuel; the agent working the thread is the product. Without the agent it is a rules page; without the playbook it is ChatGPT guessing, and I saw a strong model guess wrong. You send every mail yourself on purpose, because the complaint should come from the passenger's own address.
