### Confidence determines Speed vs. Quality · #41 · `high` · business · CLAIM
- **What it is:** A decision-making heuristic: when you have high confidence in the right direction, optimize for speed of execution; when confidence is low, optimize for quality of information-gathering. This resolves the false dichotomy between 'move fast' and 'be careful'—the answer depends on your current epistemic state. Teams that always move fast make avoidable mistakes; teams that always seek more information suffer analysis paralysis. The meta-skill is accurately calibrating your own confidence level.
- **How it helps:** Directly applicable to building, launching, and scaling products and businesses. Helps prioritize actions, identify market opportunities, and build sustainable competitive advantages.
- **When to use:** When building or iterating on products; when evaluating market opportunities; when deciding resource allocation; when scaling operations.
- **Unique value:** Directly actionable for product and business decisions. 'Confidence determines Speed vs. Quality' provides a specific framework that has been validated in real-world startup and enterprise contexts.
- **Limits:** Business models are context-dependent—what worked in one market may fail in another. Survivorship bias colors our understanding. Execution matters as much as strategy.
- **Foundations:** Rooted in signal detection theory, Bayesian decision theory, and satisficing (Simon). Draws on confidence calibration research in cognitive psychology and Type I/II error trade-offs in statistics. Informed by lean manufacturing's quality control philosophy and agile development's iterative validation approach.

### First-mover advantage vs First-mover disadvantage · #52 · `high` · business · CLAIM
- **What it is:** The tension between the benefits of entering a market early (brand recognition, customer lock-in, network effects, patent protection) and the costs (educating the market, making mistakes others learn from, committing to technology before standards settle). Research shows first-mover advantage is far less reliable than commonly believed—fast followers often win by learning from pioneers' mistakes. The key variable is whether your market has strong network effects or switching costs.

### Innovator's DNA (Discovery Skills) · #61 · `llm-high` · business · CLAIM
- **What it is:** From Clayton Christensen and colleagues' research identifying five behavioral skills that distinguish innovative entrepreneurs: associating (connecting unrelated ideas), questioning (challenging assumptions), observing (watching the world carefully), networking (with diverse people), and experimenting (trying new experiences). The key insight is that innovation is a learnable behavior pattern, not an innate trait—and the most important skill is associating, which depends on having a large and diverse stock of experiences to draw from.

### Open Platform vs Closed Platform · #75 · `high` · business · CLAIM
- **What it is:** The strategic choice between allowing third-party developers to build on your platform (open) or maintaining full control over the experience (closed). Open platforms grow faster through network effects and ecosystem innovation but sacrifice quality control and can be commoditized. Closed platforms maintain quality and margins but limit innovation to internal resources. Apple (closed) vs. Android (open) illustrates the tradeoff—both strategies can win depending on market conditions and execution.

### Sphere of Influence · #87 · `llm-high` · business · CLAIM
- **What it is:** The set of people, decisions, and outcomes you can meaningfully affect. Strategically, distinguishing your sphere of influence from your sphere of concern (things you care about but can't affect) is essential for effective allocation of energy and attention. The Stoic version: focus only on what you can control. The pragmatic version: the fastest way to expand your sphere of influence is to demonstrate competence within its current boundaries.
