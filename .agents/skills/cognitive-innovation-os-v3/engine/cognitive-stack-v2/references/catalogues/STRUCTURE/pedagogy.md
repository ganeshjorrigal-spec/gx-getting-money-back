### Coaching Continuum (Directive to Facilitative Spectrum) · #2087 · `high` · pedagogy · CLAIM
- **What it is:** Maps the full spectrum from fully directive (telling what to do) to fully facilitative (questions helping learners self-discover). Between these lie demonstration, guided discovery, Socratic questioning, and reflective partnership. The critical skill is matching position to situation: directive coaching for safety-critical skills, true beginners, and time-critical moments; facilitative coaching for developing independent thinking, experienced learners, and context-dependent problems. Most coaches default to one end regardless of context—the expert moves fluidly.
- **How it helps:** This gives managers and educators a framework for adjusting approach to match needs. The most common error is using the approach comfortable for the coach rather than effective for the learner. Knowing your default is the first step toward developing range. In organizations, the continuum explains why the same leadership style produces great results with some teams and terrible results with others—fit matters more than style.
- **When to use:** When consciously choosing how to intervene in someone's development. When your current coaching approach is not producing expected results. When managing a team with diverse skill levels needing different support. When training new coaches who need to expand beyond their default approach.
- **Unique value:** Provides a spectrum rather than binary choice between telling and asking. Situational matching criteria (safety, experience, time pressure) give concrete guidance. Makes coaching approach a deliberate, strategic choice rather than personality default.
- **Limits:** Moving along the continuum requires skill in multiple approaches. Learners may resist the approach that would benefit them most. Cultural contexts influence which approaches are acceptable. The 'right' position is often ambiguous in practice.
- **Foundations:** Grounded in humanistic coaching theory (Rogers' non-directive counseling), behavioral skill training, and adult learning theory. Draws on situational leadership theory (Hersey & Blanchard), self-determination theory, and cognitive apprenticeship research. Influenced by GROW model coaching methodology and the directive-facilitative spectrum in educational psychology.
