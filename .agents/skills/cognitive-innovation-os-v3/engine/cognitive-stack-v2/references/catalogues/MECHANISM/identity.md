### Self-Discrepancy Theory (Higgins) · #1500 · `llm-high` · identity · CLAIM
- **What it is:** E. Tory Higgins' self-discrepancy theory identifies three self-representations—actual self (who you are), ideal self (who you'd like to be), and ought self (who you believe you should be)—and predicts that discrepancies between them produce specific emotional disturbances. Actual-ideal discrepancy (falling short of your aspirations) produces dejection-related emotions: sadness, disappointment, shame. Actual-ought discrepancy (failing to meet obligations or standards) produces agitation-related emotions: anxiety, guilt, fear. The theory explains why two people experiencing the same 'failure' can have radically different emotional responses—one feels sad (ideal-self discrepancy) while the other feels anxious (ought-self discrepancy).
- **How it helps:** For knowledge workers, this model is diagnostic gold: when you're emotionally distressed, identifying which discrepancy is activated tells you exactly what needs attention. Use it to: (1) when feeling sad about your career, ask 'Am I falling short of my ideal?' and work on either closing the gap or revising the ideal, (2) when feeling anxious, ask 'Am I failing an obligation or standard?' and clarify whose standard it is and whether it's legitimate, (3) recognize that much professional distress comes from actual-ought discrepancies involving internalized parental or social expectations that may not be your own, (4) help team members by diagnosing whether their distress is.
- **When to use:** When experiencing negative emotions about your career or life and wanting to diagnose the specific source. When coaching someone through professional distress. When personal standards produce chronic dissatisfaction and you need to determine which self-representation to adjust. When anxiety and sadness coexist and you need to untangle their separate causes.
- **Unique value:** Maps specific emotional experiences to specific cognitive discrepancies, enabling precise intervention. Distinguishes between aspiration-failure (sadness) and obligation-failure (anxiety) as fundamentally different experiences requiring different responses. Accounts for whose perspective creates the discrepancy—your own vs. others'.
- **Limits:** The clean mapping of discrepancy to emotion may oversimplify—people often experience mixed emotions. Cultural variation in ideal vs. ought emphasis (individualistic vs. collectivistic cultures) may alter the dynamics. The theory focuses on cognition and may underemphasize biological or situational contributors to emotion.
- **Foundations:** Founded on Tory Higgins's cognitive social psychology research on self-guide discrepancies. Draws on self-regulation theory, affect-as-information research, clinical psychology's distinctions between depression-linked ought-self gaps and anxiety-linked ideal-self gaps.
