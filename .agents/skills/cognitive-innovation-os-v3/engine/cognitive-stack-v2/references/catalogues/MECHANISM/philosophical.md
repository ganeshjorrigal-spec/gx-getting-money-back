### A Cascade of Homunculi · #547 · `high` · philosophical · CLAIM
- **What it is:** Dennett's strategy for explaining cognition without invoking an all-knowing inner observer (the 'homunculus' problem): instead of explaining perception by positing a 'little person' inside the brain who watches the inner screen, break the homunculus into DUMBER sub-agents, and those into even dumber sub-sub-agents, cascading down until you reach mechanisms simple enough to be implemented by neurons. Each level is less intelligent than the one above, and the bottom level requires no intelligence at all—just mechanical response. The cascade dissolves the mystery of intelligence by showing that COMPLEX competence can be built from layers of SIMPLE competence.
- **How it helps:** When facing a complex capability that seems to require a magical intelligence: decompose it into a cascade of increasingly simple sub-capabilities until you reach operations that require no intelligence. This is the fundamental strategy of computer science (subroutines), organizational design (hierarchy of roles), and engineering (system decomposition). The diagnostic: if your explanation of a capability requires an equally capable explainer inside the system, you haven't explained anything—you've just pushed the mystery inside. Keep decomposing until the sub-components are mechanistically explicable.
- **When to use:** When explanations of intelligence or capability are circular (invoking equally intelligent sub-components); when complex systems need decomposition into explicable sub-systems; when the homunculus problem (explaining mind by positing another mind inside) needs resolution; when understanding how complex competence emerges from simple components.
- **Unique value:** Solves the fundamental problem of explaining intelligence WITHOUT invoking intelligence as an unexplained given: decompose into dumber and dumber sub-agents until you reach mechanism. This is both a philosophical dissolution of the mind-body problem and a practical engineering strategy for building complex systems.
- **Limits:** The cascade may not fully explain consciousness or subjective experience. The 'bottom level' of neural mechanism is itself complex. Not all cognitive phenomena may decompose cleanly. The approach is more compelling for some cognitive functions than others.
- **Foundations:** Rooted in Dennett's philosophy of mind, cognitive science of consciousness, and homunculus fallacy critique. Draws on Ryle's category error analysis, Artificial Intelligence debates (Turing), and neurological case studies. The cascade of homunculi is an infinite regress argument against naive Cartesian Theatre explanations of consciousness.

### Memes · #569 · `llm-high` · philosophical · CLAIM
- **What it is:** Richard Dawkins's concept (from 'The Selfish Gene') of cultural units that replicate, mutate, and undergo selection analogous to genes: ideas, tunes, fashions, catchphrases, and practices that spread from mind to mind through imitation. The key insight: memes replicate based on their OWN fitness (ability to spread and persist in minds) rather than their truth or usefulness to the host. Catchy but false ideas can outcompete accurate but boring ones. Viral content spreads not because it's true but because it's MEMETICALLY FIT—emotionally engaging, easily remembered, and socially rewarded to share. Memetic fitness and truth are independent variables.
- **How it helps:** Evaluate ideas through BOTH truth AND memetic fitness lenses. When an idea is spreading rapidly: ask whether it's spreading because it's true or because it's memetically fit (emotionally compelling, identity-affirming, easy to share). When communicating true ideas: invest in memetic fitness (compelling framing, emotional resonance, shareability) to compete with memetically fit falsehoods. The practice: truth without memetic fitness gets ignored; memetic fitness without truth produces viral misinformation. The goal: ideas that are BOTH true AND memetically fit.
- **When to use:** When understanding why some ideas spread regardless of truth; when designing communication that is both accurate and memetically competitive; when evaluating whether an idea's popularity reflects its truth or its spreadability; when the mechanisms of cultural transmission need analysis.
- **Unique value:** Separates an idea's TRUTH from its SPREADABILITY—revealing that these are independent properties. This explains the most frustrating feature of information ecosystems: false but compelling ideas routinely outcompete true but boring ones, because selection acts on memetic fitness, not accuracy.
- **Limits:** The gene-meme analogy has significant limitations. Meme theory is more metaphor than rigorous science. The unit of cultural replication is poorly defined. Cultural transmission is far more complex than genetic transmission. The framework can be used to dismiss ideas as 'just memes.'
- **Foundations:** Founded by Dawkins in 'The Selfish Gene' (1976), drawing on evolutionary epistemology (Popper, Campbell), information theory, and cultural evolution theory (Cavalli-Sforza, Boyd, Richerson). Connects to memetics as a research program and cognitive science of cultural transmission. Highly contested in evolutionary biology and anthropology.
