### Sense of Place and Belonging · #1567 · `llm-high` · environment · CLAIM
- **What it is:** Sense of place is the emotional bond between person and location, encompassing place attachment (affective connection) and place meaning (narratives and identity tied to a location). Strong sense of place correlates with well-being, community engagement, and environmental stewardship. In a hypermobile society, sense of place has eroded, producing 'placelessness'—the feeling that everywhere is interchangeable. Humans have a fundamental need for geographic rootedness: knowing local ecology, maintaining multi-year relationships with specific landscapes, and feeling a place is yours to care for. This requires deliberate investment wherever you are.
- **How it helps:** For mobile professionals who feel rootless, this provides diagnosis and prescription. Frequent relocation without reinvestment creates placelessness manifesting as dissatisfaction, reduced community engagement, and weak support networks. Wherever you are, invest in learning local ecology, establishing relationships with natural places, and contributing to community. For remote workers choosing where to live, place attachment (staying somewhere meaningful) may matter more than optimizing for amenities. The model reveals hidden costs of hypermobility.
- **When to use:** When deciding whether to relocate. When feeling rootless despite comfortable living conditions. When building community in a new location. When evaluating the hidden costs of frequent moves and geographic hypermobility.
- **Unique value:** Names 'placelessness' as a specific modern condition with measurable psychological costs, and provides a framework for deliberate place attachment. Uniquely connects geographic rootedness to well-being, community, and environmental stewardship as an integrated system.
- **Limits:** Strong place attachment can become parochialism or resistance to necessary change. Economic realities force many relocations. The model may romanticize staying put while ignoring legitimate reasons for mobility.
- **Foundations:** Grounded in humanistic geography (Yi-Fu Tuan's topophilia and space-place distinction), environmental psychology (place attachment theory, Scannell & Gifford), and community sociology. Rootedness and belonging research in developmental psychology (Bowlby's attachment theory extended to place) provides the well-being mechanism. Relph's phenomenological geography informs the placelessness concept.
