### Core Self-Evaluations · #1496 · `llm-high` · identity · CLAIM
- **What it is:** Core self-evaluations (CSE), developed by Timothy Judge, represent the fundamental, bottom-line assessment individuals hold about their own worthiness, competence, and capability. CSE is a higher-order construct comprising four traits: self-esteem (overall self-worth), generalized self-efficacy (belief in ability to perform across situations), locus of control (belief that you can influence outcomes), and emotional stability (absence of neuroticism). Research consistently shows CSE predicts job satisfaction, job performance, career success, life satisfaction, and stress coping more strongly than any individual personality trait.
- **How it helps:** For knowledge workers, CSE functions as a meta-capability that amplifies all other abilities. Use it to: (1) assess which component of CSE is your weakest link—self-esteem, self-efficacy, locus of control, or emotional stability—and target it, (2) understand that career outcomes are partially driven by self-evaluation, not just objective skill, (3) build self-efficacy through mastery experiences (progressive challenges with success), (4) strengthen internal locus of control by documenting instances where your actions influenced outcomes, (5) recognize that coaching or therapy targeting CSE components can have outsized career impact because CSE amplifies everything else.
- **When to use:** When career satisfaction or performance seems lower than objective circumstances warrant. When you want to identify the psychological bottleneck in your professional development. When evaluating the psychological readiness of team members for challenging assignments. When persistent self-doubt undermines performance despite adequate capability.
- **Unique value:** Identifies the specific four-component structure of fundamental self-assessment. Provides a targeted intervention approach—work on the weakest component rather than vague 'self-improvement.' Demonstrates that self-evaluation predicts outcomes above and beyond objective ability.
- **Limits:** High CSE can produce overconfidence and insufficient self-criticism. Cultural contexts that value humility may pathologize high CSE. The construct overlaps significantly with existing personality measures, leading some to question whether it adds unique value. Socioeconomic privilege may inflate CSE without corresponding competence.
- **Foundations:** Founded on Timothy Judge's organizational psychology synthesis of self-concept traits. Draws on personality psychology, Bandura's self-efficacy, Rotter's locus of control, Rosenberg's self-esteem research, and neuroticism literature from the Big Five personality framework.
