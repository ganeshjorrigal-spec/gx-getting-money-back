### Post-Negotiation Debrief Protocol · #2298 · `high` · negotiation · INSTRUCTION
- **What it is:** A structured review protocol conducted after every negotiation to extract lessons and improve future performance. Conduct within 24 hours while memory is fresh. The protocol: (1) Outcome review: What did you achieve vs your target? What did the other party achieve? Was the zone of possible agreement (ZOPA) larger than what you captured? (2) Process review: What went well? What specific moments shifted the negotiation? Where did you lose ground and why? (3) Preparation assessment: Was your BATNA accurate? Did you anticipate their arguments correctly? What surprised you? (4) Technique review: Which specific techniques worked (anchoring, labeling, calibrated questions)? Which fell flat? (5) Emotional review: When did you feel most confident? Most uncertain? Did emotional states affect your decisions? (6) Black Swan check: What did you learn that you didn't know going in? Were there signals you missed? (7) Lessons and commitments: What 2-3 things will you do differently next time? Write these down and review before your next negotiation.
- **How it helps:** Creates a learning loop that compounds over time. Most people finish negotiations and never reflect on what happened, losing all the experiential learning. This protocol captures those lessons while they're fresh. After 5-10 debriefs, most people identify clear patterns in their negotiation behavior — both strengths and recurring mistakes. The compound effect of this practice over a career is enormous.
- **When to use:** After every significant negotiation — salary discussions, vendor deals, client contracts, partnership agreements, even difficult personal conversations that had negotiation elements. Also useful after negotiations you observed but didn't participate in. Can be done solo or as a team exercise after group negotiations.
- **Unique value:** The only protocol focused on learning FROM negotiations rather than performing IN them. All other negotiation tools help you do better during the conversation; this one ensures you get better at the meta-skill of negotiation itself over time.
- **Limits:** Requires discipline to complete consistently — the temptation to skip it after a good outcome is especially strong. Self-assessment is inherently biased. Ideally complemented by feedback from the other party or an observer, which is rarely available. The emotional review can become rumination rather than reflection if not time-bounded.
- **Foundations:** Grounded in organizational learning theory (Argyris, Schon) and metacognition research. Draws on Tetlock's forecasting calibration work — systematic review of predictions vs. outcomes builds skill over time. Reflects deliberate practice theory (Ericsson): negotiation skill requires structured feedback loops, not just experience accumulation.
