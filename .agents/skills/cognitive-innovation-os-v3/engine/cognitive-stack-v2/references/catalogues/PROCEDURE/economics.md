### Double-Entry Bookkeeping · #187 · `llm-high` · economics · INSTRUCTION
- **What it is:** The accounting system invented in medieval Italy (codified by Luca Pacioli) where every financial transaction is recorded as TWO entries: a debit and a credit. Assets = Liabilities + Equity. Every transaction must balance. This system provides: automatic error detection (if debits don't equal credits, something is wrong), comprehensive financial visibility (both sides of every transaction are captured), and fraud prevention (harder to hide transactions when both sides must balance). As a mental model: every action has TWO sides, and accounting for both sides produces a more complete and accurate picture than tracking only one side. Costs have hidden benefits; benefits have hidden costs.
- **How it helps:** Apply double-entry thinking to any analysis: for every apparent benefit, ask 'what's the offsetting cost?' and for every apparent cost, ask 'what's the offsetting benefit?' In business decisions: a cost reduction is also a capability reduction (both sides of the entry). A revenue increase requires a resource investment (both sides). In personal decisions: every 'yes' is simultaneously a 'no' to something else. The practice: before concluding that something is purely positive or purely negative, find the other entry—the offsetting entry that completes the picture.
- **When to use:** When financial analysis needs the completeness and error-checking of double-entry; when decisions are being evaluated on only one side (costs without benefits, or benefits without costs); when understanding that every action has an offsetting entry would improve analysis; when fraud prevention or error detection requires systematic cross-referencing.
- **Unique value:** Provides both the practical accounting system that enabled modern capitalism AND the mental model that every transaction has two sides. The error-detection property (debits must equal credits) is the most powerful feature: it AUTOMATICALLY reveals mistakes and omissions that single-entry systems miss.
- **Limits:** Double-entry accounting doesn't capture non-financial values. The system requires training and discipline. Some transactions resist clean categorization. Financial statements still require interpretation. The analogy to non-financial 'double entry' is metaphorical.
- **Foundations:** Developed in Renaissance accounting (Pacioli, 1494); rooted in mathematical duality and transaction theory. Connects to systems thinking's conservation principles, financial accounting theory, and information theory's requirement for internal consistency checks.

### Income Diversification Strategy · #1593 · `llm-high` · economics · INSTRUCTION
- **What it is:** Income diversification applies portfolio theory to earned income: construct multiple uncorrelated sources rather than relying on one job or client. The model distinguishes active income (requiring time—salary, freelancing), portfolio income (returns on capital—dividends, rental income), and passive income (systems generating revenue with minimal effort—digital products, royalties). Uncorrelated streams reduce total volatility more than increasing any single stream. A freelancer with three clients in different industries is more financially stable than an employee earning the same total, despite the perception that employment is 'safer.'
- **How it helps:** For knowledge workers, this reframes employment-vs-entrepreneurship: the goal is a diversified income portfolio, not picking one. Build side income while employed, convert expertise into scalable products, and invest for portfolio income. For entrepreneurs, avoid revenue concentration—more than 30% from one client is risk. The model provides a framework for evaluating which additional streams to pursue: prioritize those uncorrelated with your primary source. Apply portfolio diversification thinking to income, not just investments.
- **When to use:** When your entire income depends on one employer or client. When building financial strategy for job market volatility. When deciding which side projects to pursue. When evaluating true risk of employment versus self-employment.
- **Unique value:** Applies portfolio diversification principles to earned income, revealing that income stream correlation matters more than total income amount. Reframes the employment/entrepreneurship binary into a portfolio construction problem with multiple possible configurations.
- **Limits:** Building multiple streams requires time that may detract from primary income growth. 'Passive income' is often far less passive than marketed. Tax complexity increases. Some careers demand singular focus incompatible with diversification.
- **Foundations:** Rooted in Harry Markowitz's Modern Portfolio Theory and its application to income streams, labor economics research on earnings diversification, and entrepreneurship literature. Robert Kiyosaki's practitioner framework popularized the active/portfolio/passive income taxonomy. Behavioral economics of loss aversion (Kahneman) explains why income concentration feels safer despite being riskier.

### Financial Advisor Selection Framework · #1609 · `llm-high` · economics · INSTRUCTION
- **What it is:** The advisor landscape deliberately confuses consumers. A clear framework evaluates four dimensions: (1) Fiduciary duty—legally required to act in your interest (vs. suitability standard allowing conflicted recommendations); (2) Fee-only compensation—no product commissions creating conflicts; (3) CFP credential—comprehensive education, examination, and ethics requirements; (4) Specialization matching your situation. The minimum viable advisor is fee-only, fiduciary, CFP-credentialed. This filter eliminates ~80% of advisors, which is the point—most of the industry is structured around product sales, not advice.
- **How it helps:** Ask any prospective advisor: 'Are you a fiduciary at all times?' (get it in writing), 'Fee-only or fee-based?' (only fee-only), 'What credentials?' (CFP minimum), 'Who is your typical client?' (match your situation). For knowledge workers with simple situations, consider a flat-fee advisor for a one-time financial plan, then implement yourself with index funds. The four-question filter protects against the industry's most common conflicts of interest while being simple enough to apply in a single conversation.
- **When to use:** When searching for a financial advisor. When evaluating your current advisor. When someone recommends an advisor and you need to vet them. When financial complexity exceeds your comfort for self-management.
- **Unique value:** Provides four specific, non-negotiable screening criteria (fiduciary, fee-only, CFP, specialization) cutting through deliberate industry complexity. Explains clearly why most financial 'advisors' are actually product salespeople operating under a different legal standard.
- **Limits:** Fee-only fiduciaries charge higher transparent fees. Some excellent advisors don't fit all criteria. Many people don't need an advisor at all. Quality varies even within the category. Framework is US-centric.
- **Foundations:** Grounded in securities law (Investment Advisers Act, fiduciary standards), financial economics of principal-agent problems (Jensen & Meckling), and consumer protection theory. CFP Board standards formalize professional obligations. Behavioral economics of conflicts of interest (Cain, Loewenstein & Moore) explains why non-fiduciary advice systematically harms consumers even with disclosure.

### Emergency Financial Protocol · #2367 · `high` · economics · INSTRUCTION
- **What it is:** A step-by-step protocol for managing a sudden financial crisis — job loss, major unexpected expense, income disruption, or financial fraud. Step 1 — Assess (Day 1): Calculate exactly how much cash you have access to (all accounts, all sources). Determine your monthly essential expenses (housing, food, utilities, insurance, minimum debt payments). Divide: Cash / Monthly Essentials = Months of runway. Step 2 — Stop the bleeding (Days 1-3): Immediately pause all non-essential spending. Cancel subscriptions. Halt automatic savings transfers (you need liquidity now). Put all credit cards in a drawer — cash and debit only. Step 3 — Protect essentials (Week 1): Contact creditors proactively — request hardship deferrals or reduced payments. Most creditors prefer to negotiate rather than chase delinquent accounts. Apply for unemployment or relevant benefits immediately (there are often waiting periods). Step 4 — Generate cash (Weeks 1-4): Sell assets you don't need. Take any available work (gig, freelance, temp). Apply for emergency assistance (community organizations, 211 hotline). Step 5 — Triage debts: Pay in this order: housing, utilities, food, transportation (you need these to earn), insurance, then everything else. Minimum payments only on lower-priority debts. Step 6 — Plan forward: Once stabilized, create a 90-day recovery budget based on realistic income assumptions.
- **How it helps:** Provides a clear sequence of actions during the financial panic that prevents most people from thinking clearly. The 'Months of runway' calculation immediately transforms overwhelming anxiety into a concrete number you can plan around. The creditor contact step is the single most under-utilized tactic — most people hide from creditors out of shame, but proactive contact consistently produces better outcomes than avoidance. The debt triage order prevents the common mistake of paying credit cards while falling behind on rent. Immediately actionable on day one of any financial crisis.
- **When to use:** Immediately upon job loss, significant income reduction, major unexpected expense, discovery of financial fraud, or any event that threatens your ability to meet basic financial obligations. Also useful as a preventive exercise: run the 'Months of runway' calculation annually to know your cushion.
- **Unique value:** The only financial crisis protocol organized as a time-sequenced action plan (Day 1 → Week 1 → Month 1) rather than a list of tips. The sequencing matters — contacting creditors before you miss payments produces dramatically better outcomes than contacting them after.
- **Limits:** Assumes some level of financial literacy and access to financial accounts. Doesn't address crises caused by addiction, abuse, or mental health conditions that prevent the person from executing the protocol. Some financial crises (medical debt, legal judgments) have complexities beyond this protocol's scope. The 'take any work' step may conflict with strategic career considerations.
- **Foundations:** Grounded in behavioral economics research on financial decision-making under stress and cognitive load theory. Draws on emergency fund research in personal finance and the psychology of scarcity (Mullainathan & Shafir). Reflects research showing financial stress impairs cognition — pre-made protocols restore deliberate processing during acute stress.
