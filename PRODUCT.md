# **PRODUCT.md**

Refund Genie: an AI agent that takes over a stuck domestic flight refund and stays on it till the money lands. (Started as Tickback)

## **1\. The job**

When the date they gave me for my flight refund has passed, the bank shows nothing, and the airline and the travel app keep sending me to each other, I want someone to take over the chasing and just keep me updated, so I can get my money back without spending my weeks on calls and follow-ups.

Other moments it happens:

* The app says "refund processed" or "initiated", but nothing is there in the bank even after the days they promised.  
* I cancelled within 24 hours on a travel app, the airline says the app will pass it on, and the app asks me for a proof I don't have.  
* Support moves me to a DM or a new chat, I share my booking ID again, and then it goes quiet.  
* The airline offers a credit shell when what I want is my money back.  
* The airline cancels a lot of flights at once (like IndiGo in Dec 2025\) and I am one in a very long queue.

**Who:** someone who flies domestic a few times a year, books on a travel app or the airline's site for the deal, whose flight got cancelled (by the airline or by them), whose refund is now past the date they were told, and who has already called or chatted more than once and is tired of being the messenger between two companies.

**Today they hire:** the app chat support or the care number one more time, an escalation on X that ends in "please DM us", ChatGPT for one angry mail, a friend who knows someone at the bank, or nothing at all. Most people are not refused, I think they are outlasted. In the five stuck-money conversations I had before this, nobody was refused, most just gave up because chasing cost more than it was worth.

**Two people show up in this product (actually three in case of flight refunds \- passenger, airline company and the booking app/travel site)**:

* The passenger (eg. Rohan \- the person i spoke to): the job above.  
* The care agent at the airline or the travel site: when someone calls about a booking that is not theirs, I want to be sure I am talking to the passenger or someone they allowed, so I can share the status without getting into trouble.

**What needs doing:** find out who is actually holding my money, get them to move it, and make sure it reaches my account.   
**How they want to feel:** in control without spending energy on it. Not like I am begging for my own money. 

## **2\. The switch**

**What they'd fire:** chasing it themselves (app chat, X DMs, calling care again and again with a new person every time), or giving up on the money.

The forces that matter (keep one to four, delete the rest): 

**Push, outside them (what's going wrong in their life right now):** the date has gone, the bank shows nothing, and every call connects him with a new agent who wants the whole story again. Rohan, a friend who flies often, booked an IndiGo return of about Rs 15,000 on a travel app and cancelled within 24 hours. The airline told him the app would pass the refund on, the app wanted a proof he didn't have, and he made 7 to 8 calls in 3 weeks, retelling it every single time. In 13 public flight complaints I read on X, 7 got "please DM us" and then silence, 3 said "processed" with no money in the bank, 4 had the travel site waiting on the airline.   
**Pull, inside them (what they've always wanted):** someone who speaks to them on my behalf and just keeps me updated. Rohan put it very simply and straight away \- writing the mail is not the problem, the constant follow up is; just call the airline for me and tell me what's happening. He said he would pay for that.   
**Anxiety (the risk of trying Refund Genie:** **what they tried before, or a decision that's hard to undo):** handing my refund to an app I don't know. Will it ask my OTP or bank details? Will it send things in my name without asking? Every blog says a different number of days, and if something wrong gets written, what if they close my complaint? And will the airline even talk to an AI?   
**Habit (the way they already do it):** open the app chat one more time, or call care again and wait on hold. Or the most common one, "chhodo, jaane do" if the ticket price or the refund amount is smaller.

**What the product does about each:**

* **Push:** Refund Genie works out who holds the money and the date they had to pay by from the DGCA refund rule (revised 24 Feb 2026: 14 working days for travel-app bookings, with the airline responsible), writes to the named officer, and comes back on the dates itself. The passenger stops being the messenger.  
* **Pull:** with written permission Genie sends the mails and follows up, and on a weekday it can call IndiGo's care line. After every call and reply the passenger gets a short update mail with the status, the reference, and what (if anything) they need to do.  
* **Anxiety:** no login, no OTP, no card or bank details, ever, and it is said at every step where they give something. Nothing is sent before they read it and give written permission, and their own email is in CC on every mail. On calls Genie says it is an AI calling for the passenger, with their permission, and that the call is recorded. Rules and contacts come from the DGCA text and the company's own pages, never guessed by the model.  
* **Habit:** it starts from the thing they already have, the cancellation mail or a screenshot, pasted in a browser. No app to install.

The one worry onboarding must remove: "I will explain everything again, and still nobody will tell me who has my money and when is it going to land back to my bank account."

## **3\. The core flow**

**Today (what they do now, before my product):**

1. The refund date passes; the app says "refund initiated" and the bank shows nothing  
2. Weeks pass, they check the bank every morning  
3. The app chatbot repeats the same template  
4. They post on X; the reply is "please DM us"  
5. They send the booking ID in the DM; "under review"; quiet  
6. They call care; a new person, the whole story again; "it's with the travel app"  
7. The travel app says "it's with the airline"  
8. They google the rule; blogs give old day counts  
9. They hunt for the Nodal Officer or grievance officer email and write one long mail, guessing the proof  
10. They forget the date, then try AirSewa or 1915, or give up Where it ends today: mostly "chhodo, jaane do", or the money comes back months later after a lot of noise.

**With my product:**

1. The refund date passes; the app says "refund initiated" and the bank shows nothing.  
2. They open refundgenie.app on the phone and paste the cancellation mail or a screenshot, or just tap what happened.  
3. Genie reads it and asks only what is missing, one thing at a time (for example the cancellation date, the name as on the ticket, the route).  
4. Genie shows the "who owes you" card: who has the money, the date it was due under the DGCA rule, who to write to next and who sits above them, and the amount.  
5. They confirm the details and their email, and give written permission for Genie to send and follow up.  
6. Genie sends the first mail from its case inbox to the named officer (for a travel-app booking, the travel app's grievance officer with the airline on the same mail), with the passenger in CC.  
7. A reply comes in. Genie reads it, decides who holds the money now, and the next mail goes on the right date, or up to the officer above.  
8. On a weekday, if they want, they tap "Ask Genie to call IndiGo". Genie calls, gets through the menu, tells the agent it is an AI calling for the passenger, and asks for the status, the reference and the date.  
9. After the call, Genie mails them what care said. If care needs to verify the passenger, the mail tells them the number to call back and the reference to quote.  
10. The money lands. They tap "It's in" and see the whole chase in one count.

**Things it takes to get the job done today:** about 10 steps, and the worst ones repeat. Rohan made 7 to 8 calls in 3 weeks and told the whole story every time, so in real life it is more like 20 to 30 things spread over weeks, with no end date. 

**With my product:** one sitting of about 5 minutes on day one (paste, answer 2 to 4 questions about details that were not there in the mail or screenshot they shared (like the cancellation date or the name as on the ticket), confirm the details and the email, give written permission, and calling permission if they want calls), so roughly 8 to 10 taps and answers. After that it is a few small touches over the weeks: read Genie's update mails, tap call when they want, maybe paste a reply that did not reach the case inbox, maybe make one callback if care insists on talking to the passenger, and tap "It's in". To be honest, the steps don't drop from 10 to 4\. 

**What I think changes is the shape:** the effort moves into one sitting at the start, and the repeating part (the calls, the retelling, remembering the date) is what Genie takes away. The one thing still left with the passenger is verification, when care will only talk to them.

**What must not happen**:   
Step 2: no OTP, card or bank details, or login, ever. If someone pastes an OTP, it is not kept.   
Step 4: the old day counts (21 working days) must not come back, and the wrong company must not be named as the one who owes.   
Step 6: nothing is sent without written permission, never to an address that is not the company's own checked contact, and never without the passenger in CC.   
Step 7: a reply must not get lost, and Genie must not go quiet. If a reply says "processed", it must not be taken as "money landed".   
Step 8: Genie must not pretend to be the passenger, must not ask for or hear an OTP, and must not accept a credit shell or change the booking. The AI disclosure has to reach the live person, not only the recorded menu.   
Step 9: the update must not claim more than care actually said.

Other flows (stop when the job is covered):

* A reply says the airline already paid the travel app: the money holder switches, and the next mail asks the travel app for the bank reference.  
* A reply says "processed" and gives a reference: Genie says straight that the money has left them, the last leg is the bank, and what to tell the bank.  
* Care offers a credit shell: Genie answers that a credit shell is the passenger's choice under the rule, not the airline's default.  
* Still nothing after the officers: the dated thread goes up, to the appellate authority and AirSewa or 1915\.  
* No live case yet: a 3 minute demo with a made-up airline and travel site replying in the visitor's own inbox, and a simulated care call.

## **4\. Onboarding**

First value (the moment it first does the job for them): the "who owes you" card for their own booking. Who has the money, the date they had to pay by and the rule behind it, who to write next, and the amount. The smallest commitment we ask for: paste the cancellation mail or a screenshot (or tap what happened). No sign-up, no app, no phone number. The worry it removes (from section 2): "I will explain everything again, and still nobody will tell me who has my money."

From opening the link to the first value:

1. They come from a friend's message, a post on X or the launch video, and open refundgenie.app on the phone. Removes: "another app to install?" It opens in the browser.  
2. They see one line, "Flight cancelled. Refund still not in your account?", and one button, "Check my refund". Removes: "is this even for my problem?"  
3. They paste the cancellation mail or a screenshot. Removes: "I have to tell the whole story again." Genie reads it from what they already have.  
4. They answer only what Genie could not read, one question at a time, with "No OTP, card or bank details, or login. Ever." under each. Removes: "is this some scam?"  
5. They see the "who owes you" card. First value. Removes: "nobody will tell me who has my money."

Login: not in v1. The case lives on a private case link, and they can mail it to themselves in one tap. Their confirmed email is how Genie reaches them. What we don't ask on day one: login, phone number, payment, Gmail access, calling permission, booking mobile or email, a tour. What we ask later, and when:

* Email, after the card and before any mail goes out (so they see every mail in their own inbox).  
* Written permission to send and follow up, before the first mail.  
* Calling permission and their mobile, only when they want Genie to call.  
* The mobile and email used for the booking, optional and with a separate tick, only for calls (care often uses them to verify).  
* Payment, only after three refunds have landed: Rs 49 a year after that.

## **5\. v1**

Does (the must haves: one person finishes the one job):

* Domestic flights cancelled by the airline or by the passenger, booked on MakeMyTrip, Goibibo, Cleartrip or EaseMyTrip, or direct with IndiGo, Air India, SpiceJet or Akasa.  
* Reads the pasted mail or screenshot and asks only what is missing.  
* The "who owes you" card: who has it, the due date under the DGCA rule (worked out in code, not by the model), the named officer and who sits above them.  
* With written permission, sends the mails from its case inbox to the company's own checked contacts with the passenger in CC, and follows up on the dates.  
* Reads the replies that reach the case inbox (or that the passenger pastes), switches who holds the money, and writes the next mail.  
* Calls IndiGo's care line on the passenger's tap, Monday to Saturday 10:00 to 18:00, with the AI disclosure to every live agent, and mails the passenger what care said, including the callback step when care needs to verify.  
* "It's in", with the whole chase counted, and the case link by mail.  
* A 3 minute demo with made-up companies, for people who don't have a live case.  
* Free until three refunds land.

Doesn't (not this sprint: parked, not forgotten):

* Online-store refunds (the next playbook) and event tickets (where I started; live, not sold).  
* International flights, compensation claims, hotels, trains.  
* Calls to airlines other than IndiGo, and to travel apps.  
* Connecting the passenger into the call live (two tries failed at the phone network, so for now it is a callback).  
* Logging in, accounts, and logging into anything for them.  
* Legal notices or consumer court.  
* WhatsApp and voice input.

Nice to have (only after the must haves work):

* Care numbers for the other airlines and the travel apps.  
* The passenger joined into the call live when care needs to verify.  
* Gmail reply tracking open to everyone (today only test accounts).  
* A step count to see where people drop off between opening the link and the card.

How I'll know it worked (what they do again, not what they say): they come back to their case when Genie's update lands, and they let Genie take the next step (the next mail or the next call) instead of calling care themselves. The real proof is them tapping "It's in". And if they start a second case, or send the link to a friend with a stuck refund, I think that is the sure shot sign.

## **6\. The riskiest guess**

**If this is false, the product is pointless:** airlines and travel apps will actually answer Genie, by mail or on a call, when it writes or speaks on behalf of the passenger. If they only talk to the passenger, Genie is just a reminder app, and can a reminder app really get anyone their money back? I think nope.

**Thirty-minute check, no code (run it while I build), and what happened:** honestly I could not do a no-code version of this in time, so I ran the smallest real version on my own past IndiGo booking, at night, with my permission on the case. Genie called IndiGo's care line, chose English, went through three levels of the menu and reached a live agent in about 2 minutes 20 seconds. The agent took the PNR, spelled it back, asked from where to where the booking was, and then said she needs to verify the passenger before she can share anything. Genie said the passenger will call back, and asked for the number and the hours (24x7). The whole call was 4.5 minutes and cost \$0.37. Before this, two practice calls with a friend playing the care agent showed that connecting the passenger into the call failed at the phone network. What changed because of it:

* The agent never heard that Genie is an AI, because that line played to the recorded menu. Now Genie says it again to every live person.  
* Genie did not have the route. Now the intake reads the route and the name as on the ticket from the screenshot, or asks.  
* Verification is the wall, so care can get the mobile and email used for the booking, only if the passenger ticks yes, and the callback step replaced the live transfer. So the guess is half true: they talk to Genie, but they want to verify before they share. The mail side with a real company is still not proven, the first real cases this week will tell.

## **7\. Milestones**

1. I can paste my cancellation mail and see who has my money and the date they had to pay by under the DGCA rule.  
2. I can confirm my details and email, give written permission, and see Genie send the first mail to the right officer with me in CC.  
3. I can see a company's reply land in my case, and Genie tell me who holds the money now with the next mail ready.  
4. I can forget the date, and Genie still comes back on it and sends the follow up.  
5. I can ask Genie to call IndiGo, and get a mail with what care said: the status, the reference, or the number to call back.  
6. I can tap "It's in" and see the whole chase in one count. Last: I can close it, reopen it from my case link, and my case is still there.
